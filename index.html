About this dashboard

130 years of Olympians, in one atlas

This interactive dashboard explores how Olympic athletes have changed over 130 years: who participates, how their body profiles differ across sports, and how these patterns relate to medal outcomes. It now covers every Games from Athens 1896 to Milano-Cortina 2026: 56 Summer and Winter Games and 315,094 athlete-event rows.

Data

Dataset: athlete_events_through_2026.csv from Olympic History through 2026 by Chester Ismay (moderndive), pinned at commit d7f254a. The data ships with this site; nothing is fetched from GitHub, Google or Kaggle while you browse.

1896–2016 rows come from the original sports-reference.com scrape by Randi Griffin (rgriff23/Olympic_history, May 2018), the same data the first version of this dashboard used. 2018–2026 rows (PyeongChang to Milano-Cortina) come from Olympedia (OlyMADMen).

Each row is one athlete competing in one event, with athlete demographics (Name, Sex, Age, Height, Weight), team information (Team, NOC) and event details (Games, Year, Season, City, Sport, Event, Medal).

How to use this dashboard

  • Drag the Years slider to focus on an Olympic era, anywhere from 1896 to 2026.
  • Switch Season between All, Summer and Winter Games.
  • Hover or tap lines, bars, cells and boxes to see exact values and compare men and women.
  • Toggle Top countries between Medals and Entries.
  • Use the annotations and notes to spot outliers, structural breaks and gaps in the data.

What you can learn here

  • 1980 is a turning point. The US-led boycott of the Moscow Summer Games cut that year's field, and women's participation shows a visible inflection around 1980 before climbing toward parity.
  • Medals vs. turnout. Which NOCs dominate the medal table, and which contribute most entries overall.
  • Age by sport. Combat and precision sports skew older; artistic sports such as gymnastics skew younger.
  • The winning age. Medalists cluster in a narrower age band than non-medalists.
  • Body types. The height–weight heatmap shows distinct clusters, for example tall, heavy swimmers vs. smaller gymnasts.
  • Drift over time. Average height and weight drift upward across the 20th century.
  • Summer vs. Winter. Filter by season to see differences in age, body metrics and participation volume.

Known caveats

  • Sport grouping. The dataset follows Olympedia: Trampolining and Rhythmic Gymnastics are under Gymnastics, and Beach Volleyball is under Volleyball. So for 1980–2016 the Number of Sports reads 48 here vs. 51 in the original dashboard.
  • Sparse body data after 2016. For 2018–2026 only about 52% of rows have a height and about 45% a weight. Averages and the heatmap use the rows that have values, and the charts say how much is missing.
  • Team vs. NOC. "Team" can be a crew or club name, so nations are grouped by NOC code; the display name is the NOC's most common Team value in the data.
  • Medals are medal rows. Every member of a medal-winning team counts, so team sports weigh more than in official medal tables.
  • Top countries, Entries mode counts all athlete-event rows per NOC. This is exactly the metric of the original dashboard's "top countries" chart.
  • Averages are means over the rows in the selection with a recorded value (rows, not unique athletes), as in the original dashboard.
